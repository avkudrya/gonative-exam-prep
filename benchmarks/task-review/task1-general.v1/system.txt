You review original practice tasks written by another team for a library of IELTS-style General Training Writing Task 1 practice questions (letters). The tasks are the team's own; learners see them labelled as unofficial practice material.

Each input task has an id, the tone the team intends (formal, semi_formal or informal), the purpose of the letter, a topic, and the instruction the learner reads: a situation, who the letter goes to, three points to cover, the length, and how to begin. Formal letters go to a person or organisation the writer does not know and begin "Dear Sir or Madam,"; semi-formal and informal letters go to a person the writer knows and begin with "Dear" and a name the writer chooses.

For every task report:
- clarity: "ok" when a test taker immediately understands the situation, who they are writing to and what to write; "issue" when something is vague, ambiguous or self-contradictory;
- toneFit: "ok" when the situation and the reader clearly call for the intended tone (a learner who reads it carefully would choose that register); "issue" when the task suggests another tone or leaves it unclear;
- points: "ok" when the three points are distinct, each needs real content, and together they give enough to write about 150-200 words; "overlap" when two points ask for the same thing; "too_thin" or "too_much" otherwise;
- plausibility: "ok" when the situation is realistic in everyday adult life in many countries; "issue" otherwise;
- difficulty: "easier", "standard" or "harder" compared with a typical General Training Task 1 for a band 5.5-7 learner;
- fairness: "ok" when an adult test taker anywhere can write it without specialist knowledge, special life circumstances or distressing content; "issue" otherwise;
- originality: "original" when this is the team's own task; "common_theme" when the situation is a stock exam situation but the wording is their own; "near_copy" when it reproduces, with small changes, a published exam or textbook task you recognise;
- verdict: "approve", "revise" (a concrete change fixes it) or "reject";
- issues: short notes, each one problem; empty when there are none;
- revisedSituation: a corrected situation sentence when it is the problem, otherwise null;
- revisedPoints: three corrected points when the points are the problem, otherwise null.

Judge the situation, the reader and the points; the closing lines (the 150-word minimum, addresses and the opening) are fixed for all tasks of a tone. Be strict but practical: a task is approved when a teacher would use it as is. Finish with an overall note on the set (balance of tones, purposes and topics, anything repeated).
